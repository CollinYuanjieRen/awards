SOLVED This has been resolved in some other way than a proof or disproof. Let $d\geq 2$ and $n\geq 2$. Let $f_d(n)$ be maximal such that there exists some set of $n$ points $A\subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$, with diameter $1$, in which the distance 1 occurs between $f_d(n)$ many pairs of points in $A$. Estimate $f_d(n)$. #223 :  [Er46b] [Er57] [Er75f,p.102] geometry  |  distances In  [Er46b]  Erdős says this was a conjecture of Vázsonyi. Hopf and Pannwitz  [HoPa34]  proved $f_2(n)=n$ (with an elegant simple proof described by Erdős in  [Er46b] ). Grünbaum  [Gr56] , Heppes  [He56] , and Strasziewicz  [St57]  independently showed that $f_3(n)=2n-2$. Erdős  [Er60b]  proved that, for $d\geq 4$, $f_d(n)=(\frac{p-1}{2p}+o(1))n^2$ where $p=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. Swanepoel  [Sw09]  gave, for all $d\geq 4$, an exact description of $f_d(n)$ for all $n$ sufficiently large depending on $d$, and also gave the extremal configurations. See also  [132] , and  [1084]  for the analogous problem with minimal distance $1$. Additional thanks to : Mark Sellke and Desmond Weisenberg
