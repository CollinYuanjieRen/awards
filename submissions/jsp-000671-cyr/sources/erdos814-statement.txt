PROVED This has been solved in the affirmative. Let $k\geq 2$ and $G$ be a graph with $n\geq k-1$ vertices and\[(k-1)(n-k+2)+\binom{k-2}{2}+1\]edges. Does there exist some $c_k>0$ such that $G$ must contain an induced subgraph on at most $(1-c_k)n$ vertices with minimum degree at least $k$? #814 :  [EFRS90] [Er91] [Er93,p.344] graph theory The case $k=3$ was a problem of Erdős and Hajnal  [Er91] . The question for general $k$ was a conjecture of Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau, and Schelp  [EFRS90] , who proved that such a subgraph exists with at most $n-c_k\sqrt{n}$ vertices. Mousset, Noever, and Skorić  [MNS17]  improved this to\[n-c_k\frac{n}{\log n}.\]The full conjecture was proved by Sauermann  [Sa19] , who proved this with $c_k \gg 1/k^3$.
